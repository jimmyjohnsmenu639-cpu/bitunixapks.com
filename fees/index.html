Bitunix fees

Bitunix charges no fee to download the app and no monthly fee to hold an account. You pay when you trade, and the rate depends on whether your order adds liquidity or takes it, plus your VIP level. Here are the exact rates, how they compare with Binance, and what actually moves your real cost.

Trading fees

Spot and futures rates

The two rates that matter, and the difference between them.

MarketMakerTaker
Spot0.08%0.10%
Futures0.02%0.06%

Maker vs taker, in plain words: a maker order sits on the order book waiting to be filled, so it adds liquidity and gets the cheaper rate. A taker order fills immediately against an existing order, so it takes liquidity and costs more. In practice, a limit order you leave sitting is usually a maker order, while a market order you fire off instantly is a taker order. If you trade often, that difference adds up, which is why patient limit orders are the simplest way to pay less without changing anything else about how you trade.

Futures are much cheaper than spot in percentage terms, which is normal across exchanges: futures traders turn over far larger volumes, often with leverage, so the rate per trade is lower even though the total risk is higher. Do not read a low futures fee as "cheap trading". Leverage multiplies losses exactly as it multiplies gains.

The honest verdict: Bitunix is cheaper for spot makers (0.08% against 0.10%), Binance is slightly cheaper for futures takers (0.055% against 0.06%), and the rest is tied. For a casual trader placing a few orders a week, the difference is pennies and should not decide which exchange you use. What matters more is your VIP tier and whether you mostly place maker or taker orders, because those move your rate far more than the gap between these two exchanges. Choose on features, liquidity and trust, not on a 0.005% difference.

VIP tiers

Pay less as you trade more

Bitunix runs VIP tiers that reduce your maker and taker rates as you grow on the platform. You qualify through any of three routes: your spot trading volume over the last 30 days, your futures trading volume over the last 30 days, or your account balance. Higher volume or a larger balance puts you in a higher tier, and a higher tier means lower fees on every trade after that.

Two things worth knowing before you chase a tier. First, the exact thresholds are published by Bitunix and can change, so check your current tier and the next threshold inside the app rather than trusting a number quoted on any guide site, including this one. Second, never trade extra volume just to reach a tier. The fees you save are a fraction of the trading losses you can rack up by forcing trades you would not otherwise make. Tiers should reward volume you were going to trade anyway, not create it.

Your real cost

What actually decides what you pay

  • Maker vs taker. Limit orders that rest on the book usually earn the maker rate. Market orders pay the taker rate. This single habit is the biggest lever most traders have.
  • VIP level. Your 30-day spot volume, futures volume or account balance sets your tier, and your tier sets your rate. Check where you stand in the app before assuming the base rate applies to you.
  • Referral discounts. Signing up with a referral code can cut your fees further. See our welcome bonus guide for how the current offer works.
  • Withdrawals. You need at least Level 1 KYC before you can withdraw. The network fee on a withdrawal varies by coin and is set by the network, not by Bitunix, so it rises and falls with congestion. There is no single flat number to quote, and any guide that gives you one is guessing.
  • App vs website. The fee schedule is identical on the app and the web platform. Trade wherever you are more comfortable; the cost is the same.
