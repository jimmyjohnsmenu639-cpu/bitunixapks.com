The details

What the evidence says

Regulation — the real story

Bitunix holds money-services registrations in some jurisdictions, but it is not supervised by any top-tier financial regulator. This is normal for derivatives-focused crypto exchanges (many competitors are structured the same way), but it means consumer protection is weaker than with a licensed broker: if something goes wrong, you have limited legal recourse.

Security track record

As of September 2026, no public hack or insolvency event has been reported. The exchange uses cold storage for most funds, offers 2FA, anti-phishing codes and withdrawal address whitelisting, and publishes proof of reserves. That's a solid operational baseline — though remember, PoR snapshots don't guarantee full solvency.

The bonus question

Bitunix advertises up to 5,500 USDT in newcomer bonuses. These are real but heavily tiered: the headline number requires large deposits and trading volume. A typical new user realistically unlocks a small fraction. Treat bonuses as a perk, never as a reason to deposit more than planned.

Bottom line

Bitunix looks like a legitimate mid-tier exchange, not a scam — but "not a scam" and "risk-free" are very different things. Offshore status + high leverage + young history = a platform to use carefully, not blindly.
